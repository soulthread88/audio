EPIC FURY: Night Over Hormuz — Episode 2, The Mathematics of Decapitation
Narration body text set (p01–p13)

[p01] Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center, March 1, 3:12 AM
CIA's Iran Operations Center. Dr. Leila Shirazi stared at the electromagnetic signal monitor for the Fordow nuclear site. On a channel that should have gone silent after the strike, a signal pulsed every 5.7 seconds like a heartbeat. It was the signature of spinning centrifuges.

[p02] Tehran · IRGC Emergency Command Bunker, March 1, 11:58 AM
The late Khamenei's second son, Mojtaba Khamenei, was named the new Supreme Leader. Amid controversy over his insufficient clerical rank, he issued the order from an underground bunker: 'Execute Promise of Truth 4 — now.' The IRGC's missile commander had already been waiting for it.

[p03] Bahrain · Mina Salman Port, March 1, 2:30 PM
IRGC ballistic missiles climbed into the sky. Six, then twelve — the count kept rising. The target: Mina Salman Port, home of the 5th Fleet. THAAD and Patriot batteries linked up for a coordinated intercept. The missiles detonated inside Bahraini airspace. The shockwave shook the floor of the underground command center.

[p04] Tehran · Valiasr Avenue, March 1, 3:45 PM
Two hours after Khamenei's death was announced, the streets held two kinds of crowds: those fleeing, and those gathering. 'Freedom! Iran is free!' The chant spread between gunshots. The Basij moved to crush it — but some of them stopped where they stood.

[p05] Gulf of Oman · USS Gerald R. Ford (CVN-78), March 2, 6:44 AM
The Strait of Hormuz was effectively sealed. The IRGC Navy laid mines and deployed underwater drones. All commercial tanker traffic was blocked outright. Global oil prices broke $150 a barrel — the highest since 2022.

[p06] Langley, Virginia · CIA Iran Operations Center — Fordow Was Still Alive
Dr. Leila Shirazi's hand froze halfway to setting down her third cup of coffee. The electromagnetic signal monitor for the Fordow nuclear site was showing something it had no business showing. The signal cut out, reappeared, pulsed every 5.7 seconds like a heartbeat. It was the signature of spinning centrifuges. Eighty meters underground, in a facility even the bunker-busters hadn't fully penetrated — something was still running.
“Sam, get over here. Now.” Sam Farrell, the senior satellite analyst, looked at the screen and swore quietly under his breath. “The vibration pattern is regular — this isn't structural collapse,” Leila said. “This is the IR-6 operating frequency. I've spent seven years studying nothing else.” While the world burned, while a Supreme Leader lay dead, somewhere underground someone had chosen not to shut the machines down.

[p07] Tehran · IRGC Underground Bunker — The Dead Man's Son
Mojtaba Khamenei, 56. A man with his father's eyes sat on a metal chair in the underground bunker. He had held no official title, yet for thirty years he had run his father's shadow administration — and now he was Iran's new Supreme Leader. After the Assembly of Experts building was bombed, a mere formality of a vote had put him in the chair.
“Promise of Truth 4. Execute it now.” His voice was low and clear, just like his father's. The IRGC's missile commander nodded — the launch sequence had been armed before the order even came. There was one fact no one said aloud: in just two days, a substantial share of the ballistic missile stockpile was already gone. Outside the bunker, Tehran was burning.

[p08] Bahrain · 5th Fleet Headquarters — The Longest 90 Seconds
Captain Kim Junho, 29. He was in the restroom when the missile alarm sounded. By the time he sprinted into the underground command post, the screen already told the story — IRGC ballistic missiles were already airborne. Six, then twelve. Target: Mina Salman Port. Where he stood.
THAAD and Patriot fired in coordination. The missiles detonated inside Bahraini airspace. He felt his hand shaking where it gripped the edge of the desk. Every one intercepted — this wave. But the IRGC sent another. Two days into the war, and he still didn't want anyone to notice that his voice was shaking.

[p09] Tehran · Valiasr Avenue — Freedom, Freedom, Iran Is Free
Two hours after Khamenei's death was announced, the streets held two kinds of crowds: those fleeing, and those gathering. Hijabs flew into the air. “Freedom! Freedom! Iran is free!” — something that had been held down for forty-seven years broke loose before the bombs did. And then the Basij came.
Soraya Tahir, 22. A junior at Sharif University of Technology. She'd been on the streets in the 2022 protests too — arrested, then released. This time she came out again, without a hijab. After the tear gas and the batons — some of the Basij simply stopped moving. If that look on their faces had a name, it was “I don't know anymore.” Bombs don't stop. People, sometimes, do.

[p10] Gulf of Oman · USS Gerald R. Ford — The World's Throat
Captain Marcus Webb hadn't slept in fifty-two hours. His hands were still steady. The Strait of Hormuz was effectively sealed — the IRGC had laid mines and deployed underwater drones. Oil had crossed $150 a barrel. With every hour, the economic cost of the blockade climbed exponentially. That was Iran's strategy.
Sarah Callahan stood beside him. “I sent you the story about a kid named Nariman. Near the old Evin site — his mother found him under the concrete.” “Your drones made that,” she said quietly. Not as an accusation. As a fact. He didn't answer. The silence was the answer.

[p11] 12,000 Feet Over Tehran — 34 Seconds That Became History
Callsign Saba. Israeli Air Force Major, 35. Her third mission leading an F-35I Adir squadron over Tehran. Radar picked up a contact — an aging Iranian Yak-130 trainer. Not to run — that, perhaps, was the choice its pilot had made.
The engagement lasted thirty-four seconds. An AIM-120C AMRAAM fired; the Yak-130 popped flares, to no effect. It was the first time in human history an F-35 had shot down a manned fighter in combat. Saba radioed her report, her voice level. What had actually happened in those thirty-four seconds never made it into the record.

[p12] Tehran University · Philosophy Department Office — A Page Filled After 53 Years
Professor Azad Moradi opened the drawer for the first time in fifty-three years. A notebook he'd bought at twenty-one, in 1973. Its first page read: “Truth is uncomfortable. But a lie hurts longer.” Outside his window, instead of black smoke, there was singing — men and women, together, without a hijab in sight. The first time since before 1979. Forty-seven years.
He lifted his pen to the last blank page. “I am not grieving — whether that makes me a traitor or simply honest, I don't know. But this much I know: right now, I can hear those voices.” He set the pen down. The singing went on. A bomb only ever ends in destruction. A song is the will to build something.

[p13] Langley, Virginia · CIA Emergency Briefing Room — The Question She Doesn't Yet Know
Thirty-four hours after she filed her report, the meeting was called. “Greater than 85 percent probability that part of the Fordow underground facility remains operational.” The room went quiet. “Are you recommending a follow-on strike?” someone asked. “That's not my call to make,” Leila said. “I provide the intelligence. What's done with it is someone else's decision.”
It was true. And at the same time, she knew that intelligence was exactly what shaped the next decision. Walking the corridor, she looked out at Virginia's blue sky. Five thousand six hundred miles away, the sky over Tehran was black. She went back to her station. The monitor, the signal, the war — all of it was waiting.
